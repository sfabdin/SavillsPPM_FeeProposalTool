3 Building a new fee matrix in the Fee Calculator

Open Universal Fee Calculator → start a new project (or open one from the Projects Index). Work top to bottom. Look for the ⓘ markers next to tricky fields — they explain the nuance with an example.

1Project info. Name, client, your name as revenue leader, industry, and project type. Add anyone who should have access (see §4). Use Assumptions & exclusions and Notes to record what the fee is priced under — these will be useful in the Benchmarking tool when comparing projects.
2Timeline. Set the start and end months. This is the calendar window everything else fills in.
3Phases. Break the project into phases (e.g. Startup, Execution, Closeout) and give each a length in months. The last phase absorbs any remainder. (Half months are not possible in this view, but you can always half the allocation to get the same amount)
4Groups. This can be useful in a few different ways. Group your team by PPM Revenue Stream (e.g. PPM, Relocation, Change Mangement, Workplace), Group them by Scope, by Phase, etc. Each group becomes its own column and subtotal in the monthly schedule. Groups can run at the same time, and can be shifted accross time periods. Use this for overlapping workstreams where you'd want to bifurcate fees.
5Team. Pick titles from the rates catalog (the rate and cost floor come from the grid automatically). Add multiples where needed, type the real project role name on the right. Name the resource (if available) and assign each resource to a group.
6Allocations. In the matrix, enter each person's effort per phase — as % FTE or Hours (toggle top-right; they convert automatically). Click a phase to open it into months if a person's effort changes month to month.
7Assumptions. Set fee basis (Fixed vs NTE), escalation, industry adjustment, client discount, and rate lock as needed. Each has an ⓘ with an example.
8Check & save. Review the headline totals and monthly schedule. The project auto-saves, but click Save to be sure. Set the status (Draft → Submitted → Won/Active) as it progresses.

The nuances worth knowing

FeatureWhat to know
Fixed vs NTEFixed = the total is the contract, frozen when booked. NTE = the ceiling is the contract; once booked the total locks as a cap and monthly numbers are able to flex with actuals.
Rate lockHolds the start year's rates (no escalation) and shows the amount you gave back as a credit line.
Industry adjustmentLowers the billed rate, not the cost. Push it too far and a role flags red (below cost floor). This is NOT shown as a credit to the client.
Broker fee shareOff invoice = broker takes a % out of the fee (client pays same, we keep less). On top = markup added on top (client pays more, we keep the full fee).
Pass-throughFor vendor cost run through us. Billed = client pays cost + markup through us. Managed = vendor bills client direct, we charge a % fee only. (only the markup is our revenue)
Scope shiftWhen using "groups" you can use the ◀ ▶ shift on a group header to slide that whole team's staffing forward/back in months. This is helpful if scope shifts to a later month.
Fit to targetSolves the client discount so the fee lands exactly on your target number, to the penny. This adjusts the client discount, NOT staffing allocations.
Change ordersOnce a project is booked, don't edit the contract — click Change order. It forks the current contract, freezes it as the baseline, and shows you a role-level diff and the monthly delta. Approved COs roll into the revised contract value automatically.
Version historyEvery save writes a restorable snapshot. If something goes wrong, roll back — nothing is lost, and deletions are recoverable too.
Project intakeWhen you set a project to Won or Active, the calculator builds the standard intake table pre-filled from the record and copies it for the intake email. Yellow fields are the ones you still have to complete.

How the fee is calculated (simplified)

  1. Person's fee = rate × hours (hours = % FTE × hours-per-month, across their months).
  2. Gross fee = sum of everyone, grouped by team/scope.
  3. Less rate-lock credit (if on) and less client discount → Net fee.
  4. Broker & pass-through are handled separately and shown on their own lines, so revenue is never overstated.
  5. Monthly schedule spreads the net fee across months by each group — this is what feeds Revenue Projections.

4 Revenue Projections — what you can and can't change

Revenue Projections is a read-out of your saved projects, by month. It's how leadership sees the forecast. You can adjust a few things here directly; the real numbers always come from the calculator.

You can
✓ Change a ratingUpdate the confidence rating (1–7) on a project inline.
✓ Override a monthAdjust a monthly billing number in Edit mode when actuals differ from plan (esp. NTE projects).
✓ Toggle viewsShow/hide rated 5–7, broker fee split, pass-through split; roll up by client; export to Excel.
You cannot
✗ Change the feeThe total fee, team, and rates only change in the Fee Calculator. Go back there to fix a real number.
✗ See others' projectsYou only see projects assigned to you or shared with you.
✗ Edit another leader's workOverrides apply to your projects only.

Rule of thumb: if it's a real change to the fee, do it in the calculator. If it's a reporting view or a rating/actuals tweak, do it here.
