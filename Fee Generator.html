What this is.

One record per project, entered once, used everywhere. Price the work in the Fee Calculator — roles off the PPM rate grid, allocations by phase and month, escalation, discounts, broker splits and pass-through all in one place. It saves to the Projects Index, which every other tool reads: Revenue Projections for the month-by-month forecast, Revenue Reconciliation for the monthly close and the exec flash, Benchmarking and Proposal Analytics for what we've charged and why we win, and Profitability for real margin once time is booked. Everything lives in Box, syncs live, and reconciles to the same rate engine end to end.

Pillar 01

Price it once

Every fee matrix is built on the same grid, with cost-rate floors, change orders and version history. No two spreadsheets to reconcile.

Pillar 02

Forecast it automatically

The monthly schedule behind each fee is the forecast. Ratings 1–7 set confidence; the roll-up, the weighted view and the exec report follow from the same numbers.

Pillar 03

Prove the margin

Clockify hours land against each project's plan at cost rates, so planned-vs-actual and margin by month read off the same record that priced the job.

Access

Sign-in & who sees what.

Box identity, two roles

Signing in

The system is hosted and signs you in with your Box (Savills) account — that's the only login. Your Box identity is what proves who you are and determines which projects you see. Nothing is visible until you sign in.

All data lives in one Box folder as JSON — projects, rates, staff and studio baselines — and syncs live. Open a project on your laptop, and your colleague sees the same numbers.

Two roles

  • Revenue leader — sees the projects they lead, own, or have been granted access to. Full calculator on their own work; read-out everywhere else.
  • Admin — leadership and ops. Sees everything, plus Profitability, Staffing, Ingestion, imports and rate reconciliation.

To share a project: add the person's Savills email under Grant access to in the calculator, then ask Sarah Abdin to add them to the Box folder. Both steps are needed.

Manual

How to use each tool.

Click a tool to expand

Step-by-step for each module. New here? The natural path is Projects Index → + New project → Fee Calculator → save for a fresh pitch, then watch it appear in Revenue Projections. Look for the ⓘ markers in the app — they explain the tricky fields inline with an example.

01Universal Fee Calculator
  1. Start a + New project from the Index, or open an existing one. Fill in name, client, revenue leader, industry, project type and the Salesforce ID (P#) if there is one.
  2. Record what the fee is priced under in Assumptions & exclusions and Notes — this is what makes benchmarking comparable later.
  3. Set the timeline (start and end month), then break it into phases with a length in months. The last phase absorbs any remainder.
  4. Define groups — by service line, scope or workstream. Each becomes its own column and subtotal in the monthly schedule, and can be shifted forward or back in time with the ◀ ▶ controls.
  5. Build the team: pick staff titles from the catalog (rate and cost floor come from the grid), name the resource where known, type the real project-role label, and assign each to a group. Choose Grid or Contracted per role.
  6. Enter allocations as % FTE or hours — toggle top-right, they convert automatically. Click a phase to open it into months when effort varies month to month.
  7. Set the assumptions: fee basis (Fixed or NTE), hours per month, escalation, industry adjustment, client discount, rate lock. Add broker fee share or pass-through lines if the deal has them.
  8. Watch the cost-floor advisory — any role billed below its cost rate is flagged. It's allowed, just surfaced.
  9. Use Fit to target to solve the client discount so the fee lands exactly on your number.
  10. Review the monthly schedule, set the status and rating (1–7), and save. Export to Excel or print to PDF for the client proposal.
Scope changed after booking? Don't edit the contract — use Change order. It forks the current contract, freezes it as the baseline, and shows a role-level diff and monthly delta. Approved COs roll into the revised contract value automatically. Every save also writes a restorable version.
02Projects Index
  1. Scan the KPIs at the top — pipeline, booked, active, win rate and contract value for everything you can see.
  2. Search and filter by status, client, industry, leader or service line; sort by fee or last updated.
  3. Each row shows the net fee from the same engine as the calculator, with approved change orders folded into the revised contract.
  4. Click a row to open that project in the calculator. Use + New project to create one, or Ingest proposal (admin) to reconstruct a historical one.
  5. Export DB takes a JSON backup of everything you can see; Import JSON restores or merges.
Deleted something by mistake? Deletions are tombstoned, not destroyed — restore from the index. The activity log records every create, save, status change and delete with who did it.
03Revenue Projections
  1. Set each project's rating (1–7) in the calculator — 1 Booked through 7 Dead Pursuit. It defaults from the lifecycle status; override it as confidence changes.
  2. Tag each group's service line in the calculator so fees roll up to the right area.
  3. Open Projections to see every project's billings month by month, sorted by rating. 1–4 roll into the totals; 5–7 are greyed and excluded.
  4. Use the filters — client, leader, service line, industry, year / quarter / month — to slice the forecast.
  5. Read the projected and probability-weighted totals. Turn on the broker or pass-through split to see what leaves as broker share or vendor cost.
  6. In Edit mode, change a rating inline or override a monthly figure where actuals differ from plan — useful on NTE work. Export the matrix to Excel exactly as shown.
Rule of thumb. If it's a real change to the fee, team or rates, do it in the calculator. Ratings and monthly actuals overrides belong here.
05Benchmarking Dashboard
  1. Open the Comparables finder and filter by client, industry, project type, staff title, project size or project role to see matching historical rates.
  2. Switch to the Rates explorer for min / median / max quoted and effective rate by staff title, plus how often we've priced below the cost floor.
  3. Use Project comparison to line up two or three projects side by side — roles, rates, fees and structure.
  4. Carry the read-out into the calculator and price the new pitch on evidence.
Read the assumptions. A $5M fee with decommissioning isn't comparable to one without — that's why the calculator captures assumptions and exclusions per project.
06Proposal Analytics
  1. Read the KPI strip — proposals out, decided, hit rate and fee at stake.
  2. Win / loss by deal size shows where we actually convert. Only decided proposals count.
  3. Why we lose reads the loss reason captured on the record — so set it when you mark a proposal lost, or the analysis is blind.
  4. Slice discount analytics by client, leader, industry, project type or deal size to see where we give margin away.
  5. Work the proposal health watchlist — anything scored for review, plus the per-client profile.
Garbage in. Hit rate and discount analysis are only as good as the statuses and loss reasons people set. Data Entry Status shows who's behind.
07Data Entry Status
  1. Find your name in the by-leader view for a completeness score across your projects.
  2. Open a leader to see each project and the exact fields still missing — staffing, Salesforce ID, rating, service line, dates.
  3. Click through to the project, fill the gap, and the score updates on the next load.
Use it before every forecast cycle. A project with no staffing has no monthly curve — it silently under-reports the forecast.
08Change Log
  1. Open it to see a dated feed of every change — newest first, grouped by day.
  2. Each entry names who, when, which project, and exactly which fields moved (old value → new value). Saves that changed the number also show the net fee delta.
  3. Filter by person, action, project or date range — or search by project, client or name.
  4. ⎓ CSV exports the filtered log, one row per changed field, for audit or month-end review.
Scope. You see activity on the projects you can see; admins see the whole system. The log holds the most recent 1,500 entries and syncs through Box, so everyone reads the same history. For a full point-in-time rollback of one project, use version history in the calculator instead.
09Profitability & Staffing (admin)
  1. Staffing & Bandwidth shows allocation by named resource × project × month, with over- and under-loaded people flagged.
  2. Pull Clockify actuals for a date range. Map each Clockify project to a fee record and pin titles where the directory is ambiguous — mappings save for the whole team.
  3. Open Profitability for the revenue projection with burn (hours × cost rate) laid against it — margin per project, per month.
  4. Compare planned to actual hours by role and phase to see where a job is running hot before it closes.
Mapping matters. Unmapped Clockify projects show as unattributed burn. Fix the mapping once and it holds for everyone.
10Ingestion Studio & imports (admin)
  1. In the Ingestion Studio, drop a proposal (.xlsx / .csv / .pdf) or paste a fee matrix. Files parse in the browser; PDFs can be extracted with Claude.
  2. It pulls project meta, phases, discounts and the roster — people, titles, project roles, groups, allocations and rates — and back-maps each rate to the closest title and tier.
  3. Every rate is checked against the grid band [cost floor ↔ rack]. Below-floor and out-of-range rows are badged; review and fix inline.
  4. Create project saves the record and opens it in the calculator to fine-tune allocations and reconcile to the fee.
  5. For bulk priming, Import Revenues loads the Detailed Revenues sheet (monthly dollars, staffing later) and Import Small Works loads the pass-through book.
Fee but no roster? The studio seeds a starter team at grid rates — then use Fit to target in the calculator to land the number.
11Rate Grid Reconciliation (admin)
  1. Load the candidate rates.json. It is used for a dry-run only — the live grid in Box is untouched.
  2. Read the report: for each project it solves a per-month reconciling discount so every frozen monthly billing figure holds exactly against the new rack rates.
  3. Review the exceptions — floor breaches, uplifts and unsolvable months are flagged only, never auto-changed.
  4. Commit. Each project gets a reversible pre-reconciliation snapshot, stores its reconciling vector, is tagged to the new grid and re-stamped — billing unchanged.
Order of operations. Dry-run and commit here first, then deploy the new grid to Box. Run the regression tests after.
