Getting started.

The five-minute orientation for revenue leaders: how the pieces fit together, what you're responsible for entering, and what every number means. Bookmark this — everything links to the tool it describes.

The flow — where a number is born and where it goes

1

Fee Calculator — price the work

Build the roster (roles, tiers, allocation %, phases), and the fee comes out. Rates come from the shared grid; discounts and escalation are applied here, once. This record is the source of truth for everything downstream — the fee is born here and never re-keyed.

2

Projects Index — your project list

Every project you own, in one place. Open a project to edit it in the calculator; statuses (draft → submitted → won/lost) drive the pipeline and weighted forecast numbers leadership sees.

3

Revenue Projections — true-up the monthly picture

Your projects' revenue spread by month. It starts from the roster-computed schedule; where reality differs, enter a monthly override. This is the layer you're asked to keep current — it feeds the booked/weighted totals and the exec dashboard.

4

Reporting reads it all — you don't re-enter anything

Revenue Studio (slice & compare against frozen baselines), Benchmarking, Proposal Analytics, and Data Entry Status all read the same records. If a report looks wrong, fix the project — not the report.

5

Admin layer leadership only

Staffing & Bandwidth (allocations vs. contract vs. Clockify actuals), Profitability Analysis, Ingestion Studio (bulk imports with diff-and-select), Rate Reconciliation, and the data-stewardship tools (Change Log, Data Repair). Gated to the admin allowlist — you may not see these in the menu, and that's expected.

What's expected of you

  • Keep your projects current in the calculator — status, dates, roster, and fee should match what's actually signed or proposed.
  • Keep monthly projections honest in Revenue Projections — override the months that have shifted; leave the rest computed.
  • Reconcile your imported (historical) projects. Projects loaded from the old workbook show an "Imported vs. calculated" panel in the calculator. Build the staffing until calculated matches imported — or use ⚡ Pre-calculate rates from staffing to get a proposed answer to react to — then mark the project reconciled. Until you do, projections follow the imported sheet, not your fee model.
  • Don't edit the same project at the same time as someone else. The tool merges most concurrent edits safely, but simultaneous edits to the same project follow "last save wins." Coordinate ownership.
  • Check Data Entry Status to see what's still missing on your projects — it's the same list leadership sees.
  • Everything is gated through your Box sign-in; the pill in the bottom-right corner tells you when your changes last synced. If it turns red, your edits are local-only — sign back in before closing the tab.

Glossary — the words mean the same thing everywhere

Gross fee
Roster hours × rates, before any deductions — what the published rates would price the work at.
Net fee (proposed fee)
Gross minus rate-lock credit and client discount. This is the number quoted to the client, and the one reports mean when they say "fee."
Fee share / broker split
A percentage of revenue owed to another party, deducted from Savills revenue. Configured per-project; bulk imports detect it from the projections workbook.
Monthly override
A hand-entered revenue figure for one month of one project that takes priority over the computed schedule. The "currently in effect" number is always: override if set, otherwise computed.
Booked · Pipeline · Weighted
Booked = won/active work. Pipeline = everything still open. Weighted = pipeline × probability by status — the forecast number.
① Plan · ② Contract · ③ Actual staffing
① who we plan to staff (names, from the matrix) · ② what the contract priced (titles, from the fee tool) · ③ what actually got logged (Clockify). Healthy = ③ tracks ① and both stay inside ②.
Pursuit / opportunity
Unsigned work being chased. Staffing views stripe it and let you include/exclude it, so tentative load never masquerades as firm.
Rate lock
Client keeps start-year rates for the whole engagement; the forgone escalation shows once, as a credit line — not by silently lowering the rates.
Imported (locked) vs. reconciled
A project imported from the legacy workbook keeps its imported monthly figures locked for projections until its owner marks it reconciled — at which point projections switch to the fee model built in the calculator. The pre-calculate button proposes rates that back into the imported totals; it never flips the lock for you.
