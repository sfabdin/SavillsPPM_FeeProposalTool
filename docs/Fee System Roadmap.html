The Dream

What this becomes.

Deliberately ambitious · sequencing decided with leadership, not by this page
Informs decisions.
Never makes them.

Every capability below ends at a human choice. The system flags, compares, prices scenarios, and proposes — a revenue leader confirms or ignores. Nothing writes itself, nothing prices itself, nothing staffs itself. That's the design principle that made everything shipped so far trustworthy, and it holds at every level of ambition on this page.

Today · shipped

System of record.

One place where fees, staffing, revenue, and burn agree with each other to the dollar. The number is born once and flows — no re-keying, no forked spreadsheets.

Next · the record starts talking

System of insight.

The same records answer questions at the moment of decision: what did we charge last time, what happens if we win these three, which month is the trough, who's free in March.

The dream

Operating system for the practice.

Every recurring leadership question answerable in one place. Every new engagement born data-first. New service lines and regions onboard onto the same engine — without a single new spreadsheet.

Pillar 1

Price with the whole firm's memory in the room.

Today a leader prices a proposal from experience and whatever comparables they remember. The dream: while the fee is being built, the system quietly holds up everything the practice has ever learned. The leader still picks the number — they just pick it knowing everything we know.

Informs the decision
  • Live comparables in the calculator — "similar scope billed $1.1–$1.4M; your floor risk is on the two Director roles" — while the roster is being shaped, not after.
  • Win-odds context by price point from win/loss history — never a suggested price, always the odds around one.
  • Assumption intelligence — which assumptions historically break and what the change orders cost, so contingency is priced knowingly.
  • Escalation & rate-lock outcomes — what clients actually accepted, by industry and size.
Gets ahead of
  • Underpricing drift — discounts creeping wider year over year without anyone deciding that.
  • Procurement surprises — floor violations caught while drafting, not in the BAFO round.
  • Margin sold away before the work starts.
Pillar 2

See around corners.

The book already projects forward month by month. The dream is tripwires on that projection — the system raises a hand two quarters before a problem arrives, while there's still time to sell into a trough, hire ahead of a crunch, or renegotiate before an overrun compounds.

Informs the decision
  • Revenue-cliff radar — coverage vs. budget by month and service line; the trough visible six months out, with the pipeline that could fill it ranked beside it.
  • Bench-crunch forecast — weighted pipeline × staffing shapes vs. capacity: "if half of these land, we're three PMs short in Q2."
  • Margin-leak monitors — burn ahead of plan, unmapped hours, NTE ceilings approaching, scope creep with no change order behind it.
  • Concentration & key-person risk — one client too big a share; one person silently holding five major accounts.
Gets ahead of
  • Quarter-end revenue surprises that were visible in March.
  • Hiring that starts when the overload arrives instead of when it became inevitable.
  • Silent margin erosion — a hundred small leaks instead of one visible failure.
Pillar 3

Plan the business, not just the projects.

Everything so far manages one project at a time. The dream adds the level above: the year, the client, and the team as first-class objects — leadership war-games the book instead of debating static spreadsheets, and the agreed plan freezes as the baseline the year is measured against.

Informs the decision
  • Scenario planning — pick pursuits, drag their probabilities, and watch revenue, staffing, and hiring implications move side by side.
  • Annual planning mode — next year's budget built bottoms-up from the booked runway, weighted pipeline, and hiring plan; frozen as a Revenue Studio baseline when leadership signs it.
  • Client 360 — fee history, margin, payment behavior, relationship owner, renewals, open pipeline — the whole story on one page before the QBR.
  • Capacity-aware selling — the bench visible while the pursuit team is being promised.
Gets ahead of
  • Budgets negotiated from anecdote instead of built from the book.
  • Renewals discovered at renewal time.
  • Pursuit teams promised twice — the same star named in three live proposals.
Pillar 4

Capture the data we're not capturing.

Pillar 5

Scale with the business.

The dream has to survive success: more leaders, more service lines, more regions, more consumers of the data. The engine already treats the data as clean, versioned JSON — scaling is opening that up deliberately, and meeting people where they are instead of asking them to come to the app.

Informs the decision
  • A real API layer — the leadership visualization app, Power BI, and finance read the same records live. This system becomes the source other tools trust, not another silo to reconcile.
  • Ask-the-data — plain-English questions ("median PM rate across our last eight pharma wins?") answered read-only, with citations to the projects behind every number.
  • Push, not pull — a weekly digest per leader: your stale records, your at-risk months, your unreconciled imports. The system does the (gentle) nagging.
  • Ingestion everywhere — inbound PSAs, change orders, and RFPs drafted into records for review the day they arrive.
Built to scale
  • SSO and server-side permissions — any book size, any team, true project-level isolation.
  • New service lines and regions as tenants on the same engine — their rate grid, their baselines, their book, zero new spreadsheets.
  • The confirm-gated pattern everywhere — ambition grows, the trust model doesn't change.

How we choose.

Which pillar moves first is a leadership call — this page presents ambition, not a schedule. Every pillar ships the way everything here has shipped: in small, confirm-gated slices that inform decisions without ever making them.
